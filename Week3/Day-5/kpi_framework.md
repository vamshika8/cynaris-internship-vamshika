# Week 3 Day 5 — Retail KPI Framework

## 1. Business Domain

**Domain:** Retail Sales

**Business Objective:** Monitor sales performance, customer behaviour, inventory efficiency, and profitability to support data-driven business decisions.

## 2. KPI Framework

### KPI 1: Total Sales Revenue

- **Formula:** Sum of all completed sales
- **Owner:** Sales Manager
- **Frequency:** Daily
- **Target:** Increase by 10% quarter-on-quarter
- **Type:** Lagging Indicator

### KPI 2: Sales Growth Rate

- **Formula:** ((Current period sales − Previous period sales) / Previous period sales) × 100
- **Owner:** Sales Manager
- **Frequency:** Monthly
- **Target:** ≥ 5% monthly growth
- **Type:** Lagging Indicator

### KPI 3: Average Order Value (AOV)

- **Formula:** Total sales revenue / Number of orders
- **Owner:** Store Manager
- **Frequency:** Weekly
- **Target:** ≥ ₹1,500
- **Type:** Lagging Indicator

### KPI 4: Conversion Rate

- **Formula:** (Number of purchases / Number of visitors) × 100
- **Owner:** Marketing Manager
- **Frequency:** Weekly
- **Target:** ≥ 3%
- **Type:** Lagging Indicator

### KPI 5: Customer Retention Rate

- **Formula:** ((Customers at end − New customers) / Customers at start) × 100
- **Owner:** CRM Manager
- **Frequency:** Monthly
- **Target:** ≥ 70%
- **Type:** Lagging Indicator

### KPI 6: Inventory Turnover

- **Formula:** Cost of goods sold / Average inventory value
- **Owner:** Inventory Manager
- **Frequency:** Monthly
- **Target:** ≥ 6 times annually
- **Type:** Lagging Indicator

### KPI 7: Gross Profit Margin

- **Formula:** ((Sales revenue − Cost of goods sold) / Sales revenue) × 100
- **Owner:** Finance Manager
- **Frequency:** Monthly
- **Target:** ≥ 30%
- **Type:** Lagging Indicator

### KPI 8: Website Traffic

- **Formula:** Total unique website visitors
- **Owner:** Digital Marketing Manager
- **Frequency:** Weekly
- **Target:** Increase by 10% monthly
- **Type:** Leading Indicator

### KPI 9: Add-to-Cart Rate

- **Formula:** (Add-to-cart sessions / Total website sessions) × 100
- **Owner:** E-commerce Manager
- **Frequency:** Daily
- **Target:** ≥ 8%
- **Type:** Leading Indicator

### KPI 10: Stockout Rate

- **Formula:** (Out-of-stock product instances / Total product availability checks) × 100
- **Owner:** Inventory Manager
- **Frequency:** Daily
- **Target:** ≤ 2%
- **Type:** Leading Indicator

## 3. Leading and Lagging Indicators

### Leading Indicators

Leading indicators provide signals about activities or conditions that may influence future business results.

- **Website Traffic:** Tracks the number of unique website visitors.
- **Add-to-Cart Rate:** Measures the proportion of website sessions in which customers add products to their carts.
- **Stockout Rate:** Tracks how frequently products are unavailable and helps identify potential inventory issues.

### Lagging Indicators

Lagging indicators measure business outcomes that have already occurred.

- **Total Sales Revenue:** Measures the value of completed sales.
- **Sales Growth Rate:** Measures changes in sales compared with a previous period.
- **Average Order Value:** Measures average revenue per order.
- **Conversion Rate:** Measures the proportion of visitors who complete a purchase.
- **Customer Retention Rate:** Measures the proportion of existing customers retained over a period.
- **Inventory Turnover:** Measures how frequently inventory is sold and replaced.
- **Gross Profit Margin:** Measures the percentage of revenue remaining after accounting for the cost of goods sold.

## 4. KPI Ownership and Review

Each KPI has an assigned owner responsible for monitoring performance. Daily and weekly reviews help teams identify short-term changes, while monthly reviews support broader performance evaluation.

Targets provide reference points for comparing actual results against business objectives. They should be reviewed periodically and adjusted according to business requirements and historical performance.

## 5. Data Requirements and Limitations

Calculating all ten KPIs requires information from different business processes. Sales records support revenue and order-value calculations, while website analytics are needed for traffic, conversion, and add-to-cart metrics. Customer records are required to measure retention, and inventory and cost records support stockout rates, inventory turnover, and gross profit margin.

The current sample sales dataset contains only three transactions and does not provide every field needed to calculate all ten KPIs. The targets in this framework are proposed examples for this exercise and should be validated against actual business requirements before implementation.

## 6. Conclusion

This KPI framework establishes a structured approach to monitoring retail performance. It defines ten measurable indicators, identifies responsible owners, specifies review frequencies and targets, and distinguishes leading indicators from lagging indicators.

When supported by complete and regularly updated data, the framework can help a retail business monitor sales outcomes, identify operational issues, and make better-informed decisions.
